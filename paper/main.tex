% --- Abstract ---
\begin{abstract}
Researchers increasingly rely on AI systems to answer questions about scientific
papers, whose key results sit in tables and figures. Single-pass multimodal
retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) answers every question, including those its
evidence does not support, and shows no proof a reader could check. This thesis builds
and evaluates an agentic multimodal RAG system that fetches text, tables and figures
over several rounds, checks before writing whether its evidence suffices, verifies
every claim against the evidence it cites, and marks each claim's proof with a box on
the page. The system runs on consumer hardware with an open 4-bit 7B vision-language
model. On 150 questions from the SPIQA benchmark, judged against gold answers by a
model judge that agreed with a human grader on \nJudgeAgree{} answers (kappa
\nJudgeKappa), the agent was wrong on \nFullWrong\% of the questions against
\nSPWrong\% for a single-pass baseline, but also correct less often (\nFullCorrect\%
against \nSPCorrect\%), because its abstentions did not single out the hard questions.
Behind the identical graph, a stronger model made abstention selective. When a claim
cited the right table or figure, its proof box pointed at the hand-annotated answer
\nProofInsideFull\% of the time, and every checked table-cell proof did. The results
show that the gates reduce confident errors and that their benefit is limited by the
capacity of the model that makes their judgements.
\end{abstract}
