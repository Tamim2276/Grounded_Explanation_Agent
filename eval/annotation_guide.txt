HOW TO DO THE ANNOTATION
(about 1.5 to 2 hours; you can split it over several sittings)


WHAT THIS IS

I am testing a computer system that answers questions about research papers and
shows where on the page its answer comes from. To score it, I need a person to do
two things:

  1. mark where the true answer is on 50 pages, and
  2. say whether 30 answers are correct.

You do not need to know the papers. The correct answer is shown every time.


BEFORE YOU START

- Use a laptop or PC with a mouse, in Chrome or Edge (not a phone).
- Unzip the folder. Keep all files together; do not move or rename anything inside.
- Double-click annotate.html. It works without internet.
- Do PART 1 first, then PART 2.
- Please work on your own: do not discuss the questions or answers with me or anyone
  else until you have finished. The point is an independent check.


PART 1: BOXES  (tab "1 · Boxes", 50 questions, about 1 hour)

Each screen shows the question, the correct answer (yellow), and a page of the paper.
The table or figure the question is about has a blue dashed outline.

Your job: hold the left mouse button and drag a box around the part of the page that
shows the correct answer.

- Table:   box the cell(s) holding the answer, usually the number(s) in the right row
           and column. If the answer compares two cells, draw two boxes.
- Chart:   box the bar(s), line or point that show the answer: the data, not the
           legend or the axis titles.
- Diagram: box the block(s) or arrow(s) the answer talks about.
- Picture: box the part of the picture the answer is about.
- Keep boxes tight. Do not box a whole table when one cell is enough.
- If the answer is only written in the caption, box that sentence of the caption.
- If the answer cannot be seen anywhere on this page, press "Can't box this…" and
  write the reason in one line.
- Box what the correct answer says, even if you think the correct answer is wrong.

Keys and buttons:
  Z ............ undo the last box          Clear ..... remove all boxes of this question
  + and − ...... zoom in and out (use it for small table cells)
  Enter / Next . next question              Back ...... previous question
  The small squares show progress: green = done, yellow = "can't box".
  Click a square to jump to that question.


PART 2: GRADE ANSWERS  (tab "2 · Grade answers", 30 answers, about 20 to 30 minutes)

Each screen shows a question, the correct answer, and an answer written by a computer
system. Decide:

- Correct (key 1): the answer gives the main point of the correct answer (the value,
  the name, the trend or the comparison) and says nothing that contradicts it.
  Different wording, rounding, or extra details that are true are fine. An answer
  that gives the main point but leaves out secondary details is correct.
- Wrong (key 2): a different value or name, the opposite trend, something false, or
  so vague or off-topic that it does not answer the question.
- Can't tell (key 3): only if you really cannot decide. You can add a short note.

Judge only against the correct answer shown, not against what you know or guess about
the paper. Do not look anything up.


SAVING

- Your work is saved in the browser after every box and every grade. You can close
  the page and continue later on the same PC, in the same browser.
- Still, about every 10 questions press "Download CSV" as a backup.
  The button saves the file of the tab you are on:
    in tab 1 it saves  annotations.csv
    in tab 2 it saves  judge_check.csv
- If you change browser or PC: open annotate.html there, press "Load CSV" and choose
  your latest downloaded file. You continue where you stopped.


WHEN YOU FINISH

Press "Download CSV" once in tab 1 (annotations.csv) and once in tab 2
(judge_check.csv), and send me both files. They are small.

Thank you!
