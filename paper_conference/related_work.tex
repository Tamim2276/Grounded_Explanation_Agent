\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Multimodal Question Answering and Retrieval}

Question answering over scientific papers was made a benchmark task by
SPIQA~\cite{pramanick2024spiqa}, in which every question depends on a figure or table of
the paper and is annotated with it. Such benchmarks score whether an answer is correct;
the location of its evidence is not asked for, which leaves the verification problem
open. For retrieval, ColPali~\cite{faysse2025colpali} embeds page images as grids of
patch vectors and scores them against the query by late interaction, so that pages can
be found without OCR. VisRAG~\cite{yu2024visrag} builds retrieval-augmented generation
on such a retriever and passes the retrieved page images to a vision-language
generator. In these systems retrieval is performed once, whereas in the present system
pages and regions are retrieved repeatedly under the control of a planner.

\subsection{Agentic and Self-Correcting RAG}

Retrieval-augmented generation~\cite{lewis2020rag} conditions a generator on retrieved
passages. In ReAct~\cite{yao2023react}, reasoning steps and tool calls are interleaved,
so that each observation shapes the next action, which is the pattern followed by the
planner here. In Self-RAG~\cite{asai2024selfrag}, special reflection tokens are learned, so that a
single model can judge whether retrieval is needed and grade what it has generated. The two gates used in this work
play comparable roles, one judging the evidence before writing and one judging the
claims after it, but they are realised by prompting an off-the-shelf model without any
training, and they judge evidence from three modalities.

\subsection{Attribution and Evaluation}

ALCE~\cite{gao2023alce} asks language models to cite the passages that support each
statement and measures how well the citations hold. The claim citations used here
follow the same idea, but every citation is verified before the claim is published. A
language model is used as the judge of answer correctness, as studied
in~\cite{zheng2023judge}, and its verdicts are checked against a human grader. For
visual attribution, the pointing game~\cite{zhang2018pointing} tests whether the peak of
a saliency map falls on the annotated object; the same test is applied in this work to
proof boxes.
